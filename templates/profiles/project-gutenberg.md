Project Gutenberg is a volunteer-driven initiative that digitizes and archives cultural works, aiming to "encourage the creation and distribution of eBooks." Founded in 1971 by Michael S. Hart, it stands as the oldest digital library, offering over 70,000 free eBooks, primarily consisting of full texts of public domain books.

- **Project maintainer:** The Project Gutenberg Literary Archive Foundation oversees the project's operations.
- **Collaborative space:** Volunteers contribute through platforms like Distributed Proofreaders, which assists in digitizing and proofreading texts.
- **Needs Doing list:** Project Gutenberg continually seeks volunteers for digitizing, proofreading, and formatting texts. Opportunities are detailed on their [Get Involved](https://www.gutenberg.org/wiki/Gutenberg:Volunteers%27_Voices) page.
- **Adapting the project:** All eBooks are freely accessible and can be read on various devices without special apps. Users are encouraged to share these works non-commercially, adhering to the terms outlined on the [Permissions & License](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html) page.
